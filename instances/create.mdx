Provisioning a new instance on Joy Services takes only a few minutes. You walk through a guided creation flow in the dashboard, choose your hardware configuration, pick a billing plan, set your RAID mode, and complete payment — your instance spins up instantly the moment your payment is confirmed, with credentials sent straight to your email.

<Steps>

  <Step title="Sign Up and Log In">
    If you do not already have a Joy Services account, [create one here](https://joy.services/register/). Existing customers can [log in to the dashboard](https://joy.services/login/) directly.

    Once you are logged in, you will land on the main dashboard overview where all your active instances and account details are visible.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Navigate to Create Instance">
    From the dashboard, click the **Instances** tab in the left-hand navigation menu. Then click the **Create Instance** button at the top right of the Instances page.

    You can also go directly to the creation page at [joy.services/dashboard/instances/create/](https://joy.services/dashboard/instances/create/).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Configure Resources">
    Select the hardware resources your instance needs. Each resource tier is designed for specific workload types.

    #### RAM

    | Option | Recommended For                              |
    | ------ | -------------------------------------------- |
    | 2 GB   | Lightweight sites, staging environments      |
    | 4 GB   | Small apps, personal projects, dev servers   |
    | 8 GB   | Medium web apps, small databases             |
    | 16 GB+ | Production workloads, large databases, APIs  |

    RAM scales all the way up to **750 GB** for memory-intensive enterprise workloads.

    #### CPU Cores

    | Option | Recommended For                                     |
    | ------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
    | 1 Core | Simple sites, cron jobs, lightweight services       |
    | 2 Cores| Standard web apps, small APIs                       |
    | 4 Cores| Multi-threaded apps, moderate traffic workloads     |
    | 8+ Cores| High-concurrency services, video encoding, analytics|

    You can configure up to **200 CPU cores** for compute-heavy applications.

    #### Storage

    All storage is **SSD** for fast read/write speeds. Available base sizes include:

    - **48 GB** — Suitable for lean OS deployments and small applications.
    - **480 GB** — A balanced option for most production workloads.
    - **1 TB+** — Large-scale data storage, media hosting, and database-heavy apps.

    You can add more disks at any time after the instance is created from the Config tab.

    #### Server Node

    Select the server node (data center location) closest to your target audience. Choosing a nearby node reduces round-trip latency and improves end-user performance.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Choose a Plan">
    Joy Services offers flexible billing plans. Longer commitments reduce your overall cost per month.

    | Plan    | Best For                                    |
    | ------- | ------------------------------------------- |
    | 1 Month | Testing, development, and short experiments |
    | 3 Month | Seasonal workloads and temporary projects   |
    | 5 Month | Growing businesses with medium-term needs   |
    | 1 Year  | Established projects with long-term hosting |
    | 5 Year  | Enterprise workloads requiring strategic, multi-year planning |

    Select the plan that aligns with your project timeline before proceeding.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Configure RAID">
    RAID (Redundant Array of Independent Disks) controls how your storage disks work together. Joy Services supports two modes:

    **RAID 0 — Striping (Performance Mode)**

    Data is split across multiple disks simultaneously, which maximizes read/write speeds. There is no redundancy — if one disk fails, all data on the array is lost.

    Best for: gaming servers, video editing workloads, and temporary high-performance environments where data loss is acceptable.

    <Warning>
      RAID 0 provides **no data redundancy**. A single disk failure will result in complete data loss. Do not use RAID 0 for production databases, business-critical applications, or any data you cannot afford to lose.
    </Warning>

    **RAID 10 — Striping + Mirroring (Fault-Tolerant Mode)**

    Data is simultaneously striped for performance and mirrored for redundancy. Your instance can survive a disk failure and keep running without data loss.

    Best for: production databases, business websites, and any workload where uptime and data integrity are critical. **RAID 10 is the recommended choice for most production environments.**
  </Step>

  <Step title="Review and Pay">
    Before completing your order, review the configuration summary displayed on the checkout page. Verify that your selected RAM, CPU, storage size, RAID mode, OS, and plan length are all correct.

    When you are satisfied, click **Pay Now** to authorize payment.

    <Tip>
      Your instance is delivered **instantly** as soon as payment is confirmed — no waiting period. A confirmation email with your instance details and login credentials is sent to your registered email address immediately after provisioning.
    </Tip>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Access Your Instance">
    Once your instance is live, navigate to the **Instances** page in your dashboard and click your new instance to open its control panel. From there you can:

    - **Start, Stop, or Reboot** your instance using the power controls.
    - **Resize resources** from the Config tab (instance must be powered off for hardware changes).
    - **Connect via SSH or RDP** using the credentials sent to your email.

    Check your inbox for the credentials email if you have not already. You can also reset your password at any time from the **Manage** tab inside the instance control panel.
  </Step>

</Steps>

<Note>
  Upgrade charges apply when increasing RAM or CPU to a higher tier. Downgrades to a lower tier are free of charge. The instance must be **powered off** before making any hardware changes — attempting to resize a running instance is not supported.
</Note>
